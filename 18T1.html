SADIE: Yes, that's fine. It's 1991.
MAN: So next your postcode, please.
SADIE: It's DW30 7YZ.
MAN: Great. Thanks. Is that in Wells?
SADIE: No it's actually in Harborne - Wells isn't far from there, though.
MAN: Right, so now I want to ask you some questions about how you travelled here today. Did you use public transport?
SADIE: Yes. I came by bus.
MAN: OK. And that was today. It's the 24th of April, isn't it?
SADIE: Isn't it the 25th? No, actually, you're right.
MAN: And what was the reason for your trip today? I can see you've got some shopping with you.
SADIE: Yes. I did some shopping but the main reason I came here was to go to the dentist.
MAN: Hope it was nothing serious.
SADIE: No, it was just a check-up. It's fine.
MAN: Good. Do you normally travel by bus into the city centre?
SADIE: Yes. I stopped driving in ages ago because parking was so difficult to find and it costs so much.

Part 2: ACE Voluntary Organisation

Good evening, everyone. Let me start by welcoming you all to this talk and thanking you for taking the time to consider joining ACE voluntary organisation. ACE offers support to people and services in the local area and we're now looking for more volunteers to help us do this.

One of the first questions we're often asked is how old you need to be to volunteer. Well, you can be as young as 16 or you can be 60 or over; it all depends on what type of voluntary work you want to do. Reliability is crucial, and age isn't related to this.

Training is face-to-face and takes place before, during and after work. Often, it's run by other experienced volunteers. We don't worry about why you want to be a volunteer, but it is critical that you have enough hours in the day - if being a volunteer becomes stressful then it's best not to do it at all.

Part 4: Elephant Translocation

Today I want to tell you about how groups of elephants have been moved to new reserves, known as translocation. In Malawi's Majete National Park, the population boomed because poaching was stopped. This led to competition for food and elephants knocking down fences.

Elephants were moved in family groups. Vets used darts to immobilise them. It was tricky because they had to select the right dose while elephants were running. To avoid suffocation, elephants had to be placed on their sides, not their chests.

The project was a huge success. Employment improved for the community, and poaching is no longer an issue. Tourism now contributes five times more than illegal trade to GDP.

Conquering Earth's space junk problem

Satellites, rocket shards and collision debris are creating major traffic risks in orbit around the planet.

A

Last year, more than 400 satellites were sent into orbit, over four times the yearly average. Ten years ago, a US commercial Iridium satellite smashed into an inactive Russian satellite, creating thousands of new pieces of shrapnel. Altogether, there are roughly 20,000 human-made objects in orbit.

B

Concern about space junk goes back to the beginning, but numbers are rising rapidly. Researchers are developing taxonomies of space debris - measuring shape and size so operators know how much to worry. The alternative is a runaway cascade of fragments, rendering near-Earth space unusable.

C

Groups like the Inter-Agency Space Debris Coordination Committee have developed guidelines. These include inactivating satellites at the end of their life and lowering them so they burn up within 25 years. But so far, only about half of all missions have abided by this goal.

D

Some scientists are tackling the problem by trying to find where all the debris is with high precision. "If you knew precisely where everything was, you would almost never have a problem," says specialist Marlon Sorge.

E

The field is called space traffic management. Air-traffic controllers know plane locations within one metre, but the same can't be said for space debris. Not all objects in orbit are known or tracked consistently.

F

Moriba Jah illustrates this with a database showing two different orbits for the same Russian rocket body from US and Russian sources. He calls himself a space environmentalist, warning against a "tragedy of the commons" in space flight.
